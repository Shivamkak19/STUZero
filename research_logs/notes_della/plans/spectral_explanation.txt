Say you have a sequence, that can be represented by Ax = y. If it is an easy sequence, for example, 1,2,3,4..., then the matrix A that represents the mapping for the linear system will be very easy to solve. However, more complex linear dynamical systems may have an arbitrarily large A, or the matrix needed to learn the sequence may actually be a power of the matrix A, A^n. If you consider non-linear dynamical systems as an arbitrarily large linear dynamical system, then it holds to say that solving for A^n can learn the sequential dependencies for any dynamical system with input sequence of x and output sequence of y. Spectral filtering happens to be able to compute this A matrix efficiently through a projection of the matrix into a higher dimensional space, convolutions with the Hankel matrix, and projection back into the original dimensions.

Since spectral filtering has been found to be good at learning sequential dependencies, I am trying to see if it may also be good at predicting sequences of states in world models, ie given a state can it predict sequence of future states accurately, or even given a history of X states, can it predict the Y future states accurately.
